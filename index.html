Product Case Study

Helping older adults spot nutrition risks in DoorDash

I designed a lightweight filtering and warning concept that brings high-sodium and high-sugar cues into the browsing flow so older adults and caregivers can make fast, health-conscious choices.

Health UX Accessibility Information Design Decision Support

Overview

This case study captures how I reframed DoorDash’s nutrition visibility for older adults with limited mobility and chronic conditions like hypertension or diabetes. The focus is on surfacing critical nutrition cues without adding extra steps to a fast ordering flow.

Background

Older adults increasingly rely on delivery when they are tired, hungry, or managing limited mobility. Yet nutrition details are buried in DoorDash, making it easy to ignore critical health information during quick decisions.

  • Nutrition labels are not visible in the main browsing flow.
  • Health information is easy to miss when users are rushed.
  • Families often order together, so caregivers need clear cues.

Challenge

How might I surface nutrition risk cues for older adults without slowing down DoorDash’s fast, convenience-first ordering experience?

Approach

  1. Interviewed Rick Lopez, a frequent delivery user managing blood pressure.
  2. Reviewed CDC and aging research on health communication and decision fatigue.
  3. Walked through DoorDash’s browsing flow to map where nutrition disappears.
  4. Interviewed Judy Pintor to learn about dietary constraints and accessibility.

Solution

I proposed a nutrition-focused filter panel with large, readable chips for high sodium, high added sugar, high saturated fat, and vegetarian-friendly options. Menu cards include simple warning icons and plain-language labels such as “High sodium” instead of judgmental terms like “unhealthy.”

Key improvement: Nutrition warnings and diet filters appear before users tap into a menu, reducing extra searching.

Results

  • Expected to reduce decision fatigue by surfacing health cues early.
  • Supports caregivers ordering on behalf of older family members.
  • Builds trust by making nutrition info visible without extra taps.

Learnings

I learned that hidden nutrition panels are not enough when users are tired or hungry. Simple, respectful labels and larger cues help older adults notice risks without feeling judged, while still preserving DoorDash’s fast ordering experience.
