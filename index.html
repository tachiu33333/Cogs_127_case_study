UX Case Study

Designing a clear, accessible scheduling experience

A lightweight interface that helps busy teams coordinate faster while keeping critical context visible at all times.

Accessibility Product Strategy Prototyping

Overview

This case study summarizes the research, design, and outcomes for a scheduling workflow redesign. The goal was to reduce coordination time while maintaining a clean and easy-to-scan layout.

Background

Earlier context included fragmented tools, unclear ownership, and missed updates. We captured these pain points through interviews and workflow mapping to establish a shared understanding of the problem space.

  • Multiple scheduling tools created duplicated effort.
  • Critical details were buried in long message threads.
  • Visibility into status updates was inconsistent.

Challenge

Teams needed a single source of truth that remained lightweight. The experience had to highlight availability, show dependencies, and keep priority items visible.

Approach

  1. Audit existing workflows and surface bottlenecks.
  2. Create an information hierarchy with clear visual cues.
  3. Prototype and test for clarity, speed, and accessibility.

Solution

The final layout introduces a persistent summary panel, a simplified scheduling form, and contextual guidance. Users can see upcoming commitments without losing focus on the task at hand.

Key improvement: A left-aligned navigation structure keeps the most important information anchored and easy to scan.

Results

  • Coordination time dropped by nearly one-third in pilot teams.
  • Managers reported fewer last-minute conflicts.
  • Accessibility reviews found no critical issues.

Learnings

Clear hierarchy and consistent spacing made the biggest impact. Keeping supporting context visible reduced back-and-forth and increased confidence in scheduling.
