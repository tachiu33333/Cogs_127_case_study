Product Case Study

Helping users make healthier choices without leaving the DoorDash flow

A one-tap Nutrition Mode toggle that overlays dietary risk cues directly onto the DoorDash home feed — so health-conscious users and caregivers can spot high-sodium or high-sugar items before they even open a menu.

Health UX Accessibility Information Design Decision Support Mobile

Overview

Food delivery apps are optimized for speed and convenience — not nutrition. For users managing chronic conditions like hypertension or diabetes, that tradeoff is a real health risk. This project explores what it would look like to layer lightweight nutritional awareness into DoorDash's existing home screen without disrupting the ordering experience for everyone else.

The core concept: a single toggle in the top navigation bar that activates Nutrition Mode. When on, the app surfaces dietary risk indicators (high sodium, high sugar, high saturated fat) directly on restaurant and menu cards — no extra taps required.

Background

Older adults and people managing diet-sensitive conditions increasingly rely on food delivery — especially when mobility is limited or cooking isn't an option. But DoorDash's interface is built around speed: large photos, quick reorder buttons, and minimal friction to checkout. Nutrition information, when it exists at all, is buried several taps deep inside individual item pages.

  • Nutritional data is not surfaced during the browse or restaurant selection phase.
  • Users in a hungry or fatigued state are least likely to seek out detailed nutrition pages.
  • Caregivers ordering on behalf of a family member have no way to filter by dietary needs at the top level.
  • DoorDash already collects nutritional data from many restaurant partners — it just isn't shown proactively.

Challenge

How might we make nutrition information actionable at the moment of browsing — without cluttering the interface for users who don't need it, or slowing down DoorDash's convenience-first experience?

The tension: nutrition overlays risk feeling preachy or adding visual noise that pushes healthy choices on people who didn't ask for them. The solution needs to be opt-in, non-judgmental, and fast to activate.

Approach

  1. Conducted interviews with frequent delivery users managing dietary restrictions to understand their current workarounds and pain points.
  2. Reviewed research on health communication, decision fatigue, and label design from CDC guidelines and HCI literature.
  3. Audited the DoorDash browsing flow to identify where nutritional context disappears and where it could be reintroduced with minimal disruption.
  4. Explored opt-in framing to ensure the feature felt empowering rather than prescriptive — keeping it off by default so it doesn't change the experience for casual users.
  5. Iterated on label language, testing terms like "High sodium" over loaded words like "unhealthy" or "bad."

Solution

The proposed design adds a single toggle to DoorDash's top navigation bar. When Nutrition Mode is off, the app looks and behaves exactly as it does today. When toggled on, the interface activates a nutrition overlay across the home feed — flagging restaurants and dishes that exceed key thresholds for sodium, sugar, or saturated fat with plain-language badges.

Key design decision: The toggle is opt-in and persistent. Users who need it turn it on once and it stays on — no hunting through settings menus every session.
Default state Toggle off — standard DoorDash experience, no changes to the existing UI.
